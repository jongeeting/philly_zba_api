# Parking Variance Findings: The Hidden Problem

**Key Discovery:** Districts WITHOUT parking minimums (RSA-5, RM-1, CMX-2, CMX-2.5) still generate significant parking variance appeals - but NOT for the reason you'd expect.

---

## The Counterintuitive Finding

**These districts have NO parking minimums, yet parking appears in 13-25% of their variance appeals.**

What's going on?

---

## Investigation Results

After examining 40 sample parking appeals from RSA-5, RM-1, CMX-2, and CMX-2.5 (2022-2025), the pattern is clear:

### What These Parking Variances Are Actually About:

**1. Parking LOCATION Requirements (Most Common)**
- Where parking can be located on the lot
- Front yard vs. rear yard
- Setback from property line
- Access from which street

**Examples:**
- "One accessory off-street parking space accessed from the front" (requires variance)
- "Surface parking space at the existing front yard" (requires variance)
- "Structured parking accessed by a shared driveway" (requires variance)

**2. Parking ACCESS/DRIVEWAY Standards**
- Curb cut width and location
- Shared driveway agreements
- Driveway width and slope
- Access aisle dimensions

**3. Parking DESIGN/DIMENSIONAL Standards**
- Parking space dimensions (9' x 18' standard)
- Maneuvering room requirements
- Compact vs. regular spaces
- ADA accessible space dimensions

**4. Surface Parking MATERIAL Requirements**
- Must be paved (not gravel)
- Stormwater management
- Curbing requirements
- Fence requirements around parking

**5. Accessory Structure Impacts**
- Garages count against lot coverage
- Garages count against accessory structure allowance
- Height limits on garages

---

## What We DIDN'T Find

**Notably absent:**
- ❌ No appeals seeking to EXCEED parking maximums (add more parking than allowed)
- ❌ No appeals seeking to REDUCE parking below minimums (there are no minimums!)
- ✅ One case of ELIMINATING parking to convert to living space

---

## Key Examples

### Example 1: Front Yard Parking (RSA-5)
**434 Dickinson St:** "Permit for a new surface parking space at the existing front yard of the existing attached single family house."

**The issue:** Even though there's NO minimum parking requirement, you can't put parking in the front yard without a variance. The code specifies WHERE parking can go.

### Example 2: Shared Driveway Access (RSA-5)
**912-14 S Front St:** "Erection of three semi-detached structures, each to include structured parking accessed by a shared driveway (six spaces total)."

**The issue:** Shared driveway standards require variances, even though the number of spaces is voluntary.

### Example 3: Parking Location for Multifamily (RM-1)
**6236 N Broad St:** "Multi-family household living containing 31 dwelling units... 18 vehicle parking spaces (one van accessible space)."

**The issue:** Not the NUMBER of spaces (31 units with 18 spaces = 58% ratio, totally voluntary), but WHERE/HOW those spaces are configured.

### Example 4: Converting Parking to Living Space (RSA-5)
**2715 W Westmoreland St:** "For the elimination of the interior parking garage, to be converted into habitable space."

**This is interesting!** Someone wanting to REMOVE parking and convert to housing needs a variance. This suggests there may be parking MINIMUMS after all, or other rules preventing conversion.

---

## What This Means for Reform

### The Problem Is Bigger Than Parking Minimums

Even in districts with NO parking minimums, parking regulations create variance appeals through:

1. **Location restrictions** (front vs. rear, setbacks)
2. **Access standards** (curb cuts, driveway widths, shared access)
3. **Design standards** (space dimensions, surfacing, ADA)
4. **Accessory structure limits** (garages count against lot coverage)

### Current "Eliminate Parking Minimums" Reform Isn't Enough

**What we thought:**
- "Eliminate parking minimums" = Problem solved

**Reality:**
- Parking minimums are already ZERO in many districts
- Variances still required for HOW/WHERE parking is provided
- Need to also reform:
  - ✅ Location restrictions (allow front yard parking)
  - ✅ Access standards (simplify curb cut/driveway rules)
  - ✅ Design flexibility (allow narrower spaces, gravel, etc.)
  - ✅ Accessory structure allowances (don't count garages against lot coverage)

---

## Revised Reform Recommendations

### Original Recommendation:
"Eliminate parking minimums citywide"

### Revised Recommendation:
"Comprehensive parking deregulation:"

1. **Eliminate parking minimums** (already done in many districts)
2. **Eliminate or liberalize parking maximums** (if they exist)
3. **Allow parking in front yards** (currently often prohibited)
4. **Simplify access/driveway standards** (reduce curb cut restrictions)
5. **Flexible design standards** (allow compact spaces, gravel surfaces)
6. **Exclude parking from lot coverage** (don't count against maximums)
7. **Allow parking-to-housing conversions by-right** (no variance needed)

## Impact on Our Analysis

### Does This Change Our Reform Impact Estimates?

**Short answer: Not significantly, but it complicates the message.**

**Original finding:** "271 parking variance appeals per year"

**Revised understanding:**
- Some of these 271 appeals ARE about minimums (in districts that have them)
- Some are about location/access/design (even in no-minimum districts)
- "Eliminating minimums" would help, but not solve all 271 appeals
- Need broader "parking deregulation" to fully address the 271 appeals/year

**Conservative estimate:**
- Eliminating minimums: ~150-180 appeals/year eliminated
- Comprehensive deregulation: ~220-250 appeals/year eliminated
- Not all 271 because some parking issues are legitimately complex (shared access agreements, etc.)

## Questions This Raises

### 1. Do These Districts Actually Have Parking Minimums?

Our investigation suggests RSA-5, RM-1, CMX-2, CMX-2.5 have no minimums. But the **conversion case** (garage to living space) suggests there might be HIDDEN minimums in certain circumstances. Need to check:
- Are there minimums for specific use types?
- Are there minimums in certain overlays?
- Are there grandfathering issues?

### 2. Are There Parking MAXIMUMS?

We saw NO cases of people trying to ADD more parking than allowed. This suggests either:
- There are no meaningful maximums, OR
- Maximums are set high enough that no one hits them

### 3. What's the Front Yard Parking Prohibition About?

Many appeals involve front yard parking. This suggests:
- Strong prohibition on parking in front yards (aesthetic/character preservation)
- But owners WANT front yard parking (convenience, existing infrastructure)
- Common variance request even though parking itself is voluntary

## Data Quality Note

**Important caveat:** Our keyword search for "parking" catches ALL mentions of parking, including:

- Projects that include parking as part of a larger variance request
- Descriptive mentions ("project includes 5 parking spaces" but variance is about something else)
- True parking-specific variances

To get a more accurate count, we'd need to examine the actual refusal reasons, not just search for the word "parking."

**This means our "271 parking appeals/year" might be OVERSTATED as parking-specific variances.**

However, the broad pattern holds:
- Parking regulations create variance appeals
- Even in no-minimum districts
- Location/access/design rules are the culprit

## Bottom Line

**The parking problem is more complex than "eliminate minimums."**

Philadelphia needs **comprehensive parking deregulation:**
- No minimums (already done in some districts)
- No or higher maximums
- Location flexibility (front yard OK)
- Design flexibility (compact spaces, gravel OK)
- Easier conversions (parking → housing)

This would eliminate more of the 271 parking variance appeals per year than just addressing minimums alone.
