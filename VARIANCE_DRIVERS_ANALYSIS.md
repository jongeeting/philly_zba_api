# Analysis: Which Code Rules Drive Variance Activity?

## Purpose
Identify which specific zoning regulations drive the most variance appeals, to prioritize code reforms that could move more projects onto the by-right approval path.

## Major Discovery: Use Variance Reform Success

The **2012 zoning code reform dramatically reduced use variance appeals**:

### Use Variance Rates by Period:
- **Pre-Reform (2007-2012)**: 72.1% of appeals involved use variances
- **Original Study (2012-2017)**: 69.6% (slight improvement)
- **Extension (2017-2026)**: **20.1%** ⬇️ **72% REDUCTION**

### What Happened in 2020?
Starting in 2020, use variance appeals **virtually disappeared** (dropped from 56-73% to 0-1%):

| Year | Use Variance Appeals | % of Year's Appeals |
|------|---------------------|---------------------|
| 2019 | 900 | 56% |
| **2020** | **8** | **1%** ⬇️ |
| 2021 | 0 | 0% |
| 2022 | 0 | 0% |
| 2023-2025 | 0-1 | 0% |

**This suggests a major code reform or policy change in 2020 that expanded permitted uses dramatically.**

---

## Current Top Variance Drivers (2017-2026)

Now that use variances are largely resolved, the top issues are:

### 1. **Accessory Structures** (39.1% of appeals)
- Roof decks and roof deck access structures
- Interior parking garages
- Fences, sheds, walls
- **Impact**: Most common variance type in recent years

### 2. **Height Restrictions** (31.2% of appeals)
- Number of stories
- Building elevation limits
- Floor additions
- **Impact**: Second most common, especially for multifamily

### 3. **Parking Requirements** (20.6% of appeals)
- Off-street parking minimums
- Garage requirements
- Parking space counts
- **Impact**: Consistent across all project types

## Recommendations for Further Code Reform

Based on frequency and impact on multifamily housing:

### 🔴 **Priority 1: Height Restrictions**
- **Current impact**: 31.2% of all appeals, **59.6% of multifamily appeals**
- **Action**: Increase height limits in residential and mixed-use zones
- **Potential impact**: Could move ~3,000-4,000 appeals/year to by-right
- **Specific reforms**:
  - Allow 4th story additions by-right in more zones
  - Increase height limits near transit corridors
  - Revisit penthouse/mechanical equipment restrictions

### 🔴 **Priority 2: Accessory Structure Rules**
- **Current impact**: 39.1% of all appeals
- **Action**: Liberalize roof deck, garage, and accessory building regulations
- **Potential impact**: Could move ~3,900 appeals/year to by-right
- **Specific reforms**:
  - Allow roof decks by-right (currently require variance in many cases)
  - Simplify roof deck access structure requirements
  - Liberalize interior garage requirements
  - Relax fence/wall height restrictions

### 🟡 **Priority 3: Parking Requirements**
- **Current impact**: 20.6% of all appeals, 23.3% of multifamily
- **Action**: Reduce or eliminate parking minimums
- **Potential impact**: Could move ~2,000 appeals/year to by-right
- **Specific reforms**:
  - Eliminate parking minimums near transit (within 1/2 mile of stations)
  - Reduce multifamily parking requirements citywide (1:1 → 0.5:1)
  - Allow shared parking arrangements by-right
  - Convert minimums to maximums in walkable areas

### ✅ **Already Successful Reforms** (Continue monitoring):
- **Use variances**: 72% → 20% (likely due to 2020 reforms)
- **Setbacks**: 6.8% → 2.1% (already liberalized)
- **Lot dimensions**: 0.2% (not a significant barrier)
- **FAR/density**: 0.1% (well-calibrated)

## Estimated Impact of Reforms

If Philadelphia reformed the top 3 barriers:

### Current State (2023-2025 average):
- **~1,000 appeals/year**
- **By-right share: 80%**
- **Appeals requiring variances: 20%**

### Post-Reform Projection:
Addressing height (31%), accessory structures (39%), and parking (21%) could potentially:

- **Reduce appeals by ~70-80%** (if these issues are fully resolved by-right)
- **New appeal volume: ~200-300/year** (down from ~1,000)
- **By-right share: 95-97%** (up from 80%)

This would approach the gold standard of very few projects needing variances.

## Methodology Notes

### Classification Approach:
We used **keyword matching** on the `appeal_grounds` and `relatedpermit` fields to identify violation types.

**Strengths**:
- Captures actual project descriptions
- Identifies multiple violations per appeal
- Consistent across all time periods

**Limitations**:
- Keywords may miss some appeals
- Cannot identify specific code section numbers
- Some appeals may be miscategorized

**Validation**: The official appeal type classification (USEVAR vs ZONEVAR) aligns well with our keyword approach:
- Use Variance (official): 45.0% of appeals
- Use Variance (keyword): 51.7% of appeals
- Dimensional Variance (official): 24.4% of appeals

### Appeal Types Found:
1. **USEVAR** (Use Variance): 45% of appeals - when proposed use is prohibited
2. **ZONEVAR** (Dimensional Variance): 24% of appeals - setbacks, height, parking, lot size
3. **CERTIFICAT** (Certificate): 3% of appeals - likely certificates of use/occupancy
