## Height Variance Deep Dive

### The Problem with Original Analysis

**Original method:** Searched only for explicit "X stories" pattern
**Result:** Found only 68 appeals (0.4%)

**Corrected method:** Searched for all "height" mentions
**Result:** Found 1,259 appeals (7.4%) - **18.5x more!**

### How Height Variances Are Actually Expressed

| Expression | Count | Example Language |
|------------|-------|------------------|
| Generic "height" | 1,259 | "Maximum height exceeded" |
| "Maximum height" specific | 544 | "Exceeds maximum height requirement" |
| "X stories" explicit | 68 | "Proposed 4 stories, zoned for 3" |
| "Building height" | 25 | "Building height variance required" |

**Key Finding:** Only 5.4% of height variances (68 out of 1,259) explicitly state the number of stories. The other 94.6% use generic height language.

### Height Threshold Analysis

Of the 68 appeals that explicitly state story counts:

| Stories | Appeals | % of Explicit | Cumulative % |
|---------|---------|---------------|--------------|
| 1 | 15 | 22.1% | 22.1% |
| 2 | 12 | 17.6% | 39.7% |
| 3 | 35 | 51.5% | 91.2% |
| 4 | 1 | 1.5% | 92.6% |
| 5+ | 5 | 7.4% | 100.0% |

**Recommendation:** Allow 3-4 stories by-right
- 3 stories resolves 91% of explicit height variances
- Most Philadelphia neighborhoods have traditional 3-story rowhouses
- 4 stories is increasingly common in successful urban corridors

**Important caveat:** We can only analyze thresholds for the 68 appeals with explicit story counts. For the other 1,191 appeals mentioning "height," we don't know if they're seeking 4 stories or 10 stories. This is a limitation of keyword-based analysis.

## Corrected Priority Rankings

### 1. Roof Decks (318/year, 24.4%)
**Impact:** Highest single-issue variance driver
**Recommendation:** Allow roof decks by-right
**Rationale:** Roof decks are common urban amenities with minimal neighbor impact. No reason they should require ZBA review.

### 2. Parking (271/year, 20.8%)
**Impact:** Second-highest driver
**Recommendation:** Eliminate parking minimums citywide
**Rationale:** Philadelphia has strong transit/walkability. Developers can still build parking if market demands it. Parking minimums increase housing costs and reduce density.

### 3. Commercial (~285/year, ~22%)
**Impact:** Third-highest driver
**Recommendation:** Expand neighborhood commercial allowances in residential zones
**Rationale:** Traditional Philadelphia neighborhoods had corner stores. Current zoning treats them as incompatible uses, forcing variances for cafes, small offices, etc.

### 4. **Height (97/year, 7.4%)** ⬅️ **Major correction**
**Impact:** Previously undercounted at 0.4%, actually 7.4%
**Recommendation:** Allow 4 stories (45-48 feet) by-right in residential zones
**Rationale:** Four-story buildings are compatible with urban neighborhoods and common in successful Philadelphia corridors. Most current zones cap at 35 feet (3 stories).

**Important:** Only 68 appeals explicitly state story counts. The other 1,191 say "maximum height" without specifying. We conservatively assume 4 stories would resolve most of these.

### 5. Dwelling Units (71/year, 5.4%)
**Impact:** Fifth-highest driver
**Recommendation:** Allow fourplexes (4 units) by-right
**Rationale:** Resolves 45.8% of dwelling unit variances. Aligns with state "missing middle" bills. Traditional Philadelphia typology.

## Methodology Notes

**Data Sources:**
- 16,954 ZBA appeals (2013-2025)
- 81,617 zoning permits (2013-2025)
- Philadelphia CARTO API

**Detection Methods:**
- **Height:** Search for any "height" mention in appeal text (ILIKE '%height%')
- **Stories:** Search for explicit "X stories" pattern for threshold analysis
- **Other types:** Keyword/regex-based extraction

**Limitations:**
- Keyword detection may miss some appeals or misclassify
- For height, we can only analyze story thresholds for the 68 appeals with explicit counts
- The other 1,191 height appeals don't specify stories, so threshold recommendations are estimates
- "Partially helped" appeals may or may not convert to by-right (depends on specific projects)

**Why This Happened:**
The original analysis was designed to find specific, quantifiable variance requests (e.g., "wants 4 stories, zoned for 3"). This worked well for dwelling units where patterns like "3-family" are standard. But height variances are often written as "exceeds maximum height" without stating the proposed height, making them harder to detect with explicit patterns.

## Conclusion

**The corrected analysis shows height is a far more important variance driver than initially calculated** - affecting 7.4% of appeals rather than 0.4%. This makes height the #4 priority reform, not a minor issue.

The comprehensive reform package (roof decks + parking + commercial + 4 stories + fourplexes) would eliminate **598 appeals per year** and improve Philadelphia's by-right rate to **89.9%**, getting very close to the 90% threshold that indicates a well-functioning zoning code.

**Critical takeaway:** Always validate keyword-based analysis by checking multiple expression patterns. What seems like a minor issue (68 appeals) can actually be a major driver (1,259 appeals) when you look at how the issue is typically described in practice.
