# Actionable Zoning Code Reform Recommendations

## Based on 2018 PCPC Report + Our 2026 Extended Analysis

---

## Executive Summary

**Goal**: Increase by-right approval rate from current **80%** to **95%+**

**How**: Address the top 3-4 variance drivers through targeted code amendments

**Expected Impact**:
- Reduce ZBA appeals from ~1,000/year to ~200-300/year
- Save developers time and money
- Accelerate housing production
- Reduce regulatory burden

---

## 2018 PCPC RECOMMENDATIONS (Page 56)

### Their Key Findings:

1. **Use Variances Following Zoning Map Updates**
   - "Zoning remapping has been an important part of moving Philadelphia forward"
   - Problem: Remapping creates non-conforming uses, forcing property owners to seek variances
   - Recommendation: Continue remapping but minimize creation of non-conformities

2. **Dimensional Non-Conformance**
   - "Issues of zoning non-conformance are studied in detail. Non-conformance is most common among the generally more property with lower lot sizes"
   - **Found**: 58% of lots are non-conforming for dimensional standards (Figure 17)
   - Recommendation: Reduce dimensional requirements, especially for lot area

3. **Variance Approval Rate**
   - "Philadelphia's zoning process is unusual in comparison with many other major cities in America"
   - "There is a greater administrative engagement with applicants to bring projects into compliance"
   - Noted: High approval rate (~92%) shows process working
   - Recommendation: Continue engagement but improve predictability

4. **Remapping Progress**
   - "Remapping has been updated for nearly 5,000 acres of land"
   - Recommendation: Complete citywide remapping process by end of District Plan (Summer 2018)

## OUR 2026 EXTENDED ANALYSIS: UPDATED PRIORITIES

### What's Changed Since 2018:

✅ **MAJOR SUCCESS: Use Variances Solved (2020)**
- Was: 64-72% of appeals
- Now: 0-1% of appeals
- **Action taken**: Likely expanded permitted uses in many zones

✅ **By-Right Share Improving**
- 2012: 74%
- 2018: ~79%
- 2026: 81%
- **Trend**: Continuing gradual improvement

❌ **Still Major Barriers**:
1. **Open Space/Setbacks** - 79% of dimensional appeals (2018 data)
2. **Height Restrictions** - 50% of all appeals (our data)
3. **Accessory Structures (esp. roof decks)** - 44% of appeals
4. **Parking** - 23% of appeals (down from 24% but persistent)

---

## PRIORITY 1: REDUCE OPEN SPACE/SETBACK REQUIREMENTS 🔴

### 2018 Finding:
> "The vast majority involved open space and setback requirements" - **79% of dimensional appeals, 30% of all appeals**

### Current Impact:
- Single biggest dimensional barrier
- Affects residential development most severely
- Creates lot size non-conformance (58% of parcels per Figure 17)

### Specific Recommendations:

#### A. Reduce Minimum Lot Sizes
**Current issue**: Table 12 shows massive non-conformance
- RM-1: 67,313 non-conforming lots vs 33,337 conforming (67% non-conforming!)
- RSA-5: 181,521 non-conforming vs 79,977 conforming (69% non-conforming!)

**Action**:
- Reduce minimum lot area requirements by 25-50% across residential zones
- Align minimums with existing lot patterns (not ideal patterns)
- Create "existing lot" exemptions for conforming uses on non-conforming lots

**Expected impact**: Could eliminate 30% of variance appeals

#### B. Reduce Front/Side/Rear Yard Setbacks
**Current issue**: Open space requirements force variances

**Action**:
- Reduce front yard setbacks from typical 15-20' to 10-12'
- Reduce side yard requirements from 8' to 5' (or 3' for narrow lots)
- Allow zero lot line development for attached housing
- Reduce rear yard requirements from 30' to 20'

**Expected impact**: Could eliminate 15-20% of variance appeals

#### C. Increase Lot Coverage Allowances
**Current issue**: Open space minimums too restrictive

**Action**:
- Increase lot coverage from typical 60% to 70-75%
- Eliminate open space requirements for lots under 2,000 sq ft
- Allow pervious paving to count toward open space

**Expected impact**: Could eliminate 10-15% of variance appeals

---

## PRIORITY 2: INCREASE HEIGHT LIMITS 🔴

### Our Finding:
- **50% of all appeals** involve height issues
- **60% of multifamily appeals** cite height restrictions
- 2018 report found: 20% of dimensional appeals for height (7% of total)

### Why the Discrepancy?
- Our keyword search may be capturing "stories/floors" as well as strict height
- Height issues may have increased 2018-2026 as development pressure increased

### Specific Recommendations:

#### A. Allow Additional Stories by-Right
**Action**:
- RSA zones: Allow 3 stories → 3.5-4 stories
- CMX zones: Increase from 38' to 45-50'
- Near transit: Add 1 story to base height
- Corner lots: Add 1 story bonus

**Expected impact**: Could eliminate 20-25% of variance appeals

#### B. Simplify Height Measurement Rules
**Current issue**: Complex rules create confusion

**Action**:
- Use simple measurement from grade to roof peak
- Eliminate attic/basement exclusions that create disputes
- Allow rooftop mechanicals without counting toward height

**Expected impact**: Reduce variance requests by 5-10%

#### C. Increase Height for Multifamily
**Action**:
- Allow 4-5 stories in RM-1, RM-2, RM-3 zones (currently 3-3.5)
- Increase CMX-2/CMX-3 height from 38-65' to 55-85'
- Create "affordable housing bonus" (+1-2 stories for 20%+ affordable units)

**Expected impact**: Could eliminate 15-20% of MF variance appeals

---

## PRIORITY 3: LIBERALIZE ACCESSORY STRUCTURE RULES 🔴

### Our Finding:
- **44% of all appeals** involve accessory structures
- **2018 report: 11% of dimensional appeals** explicitly for roof decks
- Likely grown since 2018 as roof decks became more popular

### Specific Recommendations:

#### A. Allow Roof Decks by-Right
**Current issue**: Roof decks require variances in many zones

**Action**:
- Allow roof decks by-right in all residential zones
- Allow roof deck access structures up to 120 sq ft without variance
- Eliminate screening requirements (or make them guidelines)
- Allow roof decks on accessory structures (garages, etc.)

**Expected impact**: Could eliminate 15-20% of variance appeals

#### B. Liberalize Garage Requirements
**Current issue**: Interior garage requirements too restrictive

**Action**:
- Allow interior garages by-right without special exception
- Eliminate minimum distance between garage door and lot line
- Allow tandem parking spaces
- Allow garage conversions to habitable space

**Expected impact**: Could eliminate 5-10% of variance appeals

#### C. Simplify Fence/Wall Rules
**Current issue**: Height and location restrictions overly strict

**Action**:
- Increase fence height from 6' to 8' for privacy fences
- Allow 4' fences in front yards (currently 3' in many zones)
- Eliminate material restrictions (allow any durable material)
- Allow retaining walls up to 6' without variance

**Expected impact**: Could eliminate 3-5% of variance appeals

---

## PRIORITY 4: FURTHER REDUCE PARKING REQUIREMENTS 🟡

### Findings:
- **2018: 18% of appeals** involved parking
- **Our analysis: 23% of appeals** involve parking
- **Already reformed**: Reduced from 1 space/unit to 3 per 10 units (0.3:1) for multifamily
- **Problem**: Still driving significant variance activity

### Specific Recommendations:

#### A. Eliminate Parking Minimums Near Transit
**Action**:
- Zero parking minimum within 1/2 mile of transit stations
- Zero parking minimum within 1/4 mile of high-frequency bus routes
- Zero parking minimum in Center City

**Expected impact**: Could eliminate 8-10% of variance appeals

#### B. Further Reduce Multifamily Minimums
**Current**: 3 spaces per 10 units (0.3:1)

**Action**:
- Reduce to 2 per 10 units (0.2:1) citywide
- Reduce to 1 per 10 units (0.1:1) near transit
- Create exemption for projects under 10 units (zero parking)

**Expected impact**: Could eliminate 5-8% of variance appeals

#### C. Convert Minimums to Maximums in Walkable Areas
**Action**:
- Identify walkable neighborhoods (walkscore >70)
- Replace parking minimums with maximums (cap at 0.5:1)
- Encourage unbundled parking (parking sold separately from units)

**Expected impact**: Could eliminate 3-5% of variance appeals

#### D. Expand Shared Parking Allowances
**Action**:
- Allow residential/commercial shared parking by-right
- Allow off-site parking within 500' without variance
- Create parking district pooling system

**Expected impact**: Could eliminate 2-3% of variance appeals

---

## PRIORITY 5: ADDRESS LOT SIZE NON-CONFORMANCE 🟡

### 2018 Finding (Table 12, Figure 17):
- **58% of all lots are non-conforming** for lot size
- RM zones especially bad: 60-69% non-conforming
- RSA zones: 50-70% non-conforming

### Problem:
Existing properties can't redevelop without variances because they don't meet current lot size minimums

### Specific Recommendations:

#### A. Create "Existing Lot" Safe Harbor
**Action**:
- Any conforming use on an existing legal lot can rebuild/expand without lot size variance
- Size/FAR based on lot dimensions, not minimum lot area
- Grandfather all pre-1960 lots (when most of city was built)

**Expected impact**: Could eliminate 10-15% of variance appeals

#### B. Reduce Minimum Lot Widths/Frontages
**Current issue**: Many row homes are 14-16' wide, but minimums are 16-18'

**Action**:
- Reduce RM-1/RSA minimum width from 16' to 14'
- Allow 12' wide lots for infill development matching context
- Eliminate minimum frontage requirements for mid-block lots

**Expected impact**: Could eliminate 5-7% of variance appeals

---

## PRIORITY 6: COMPLETE REMAPPING FASTER 🟢

### 2018 Recommendation:
> "It is anticipated that there will be approximately 20,000 more acres by the end of the District Plan process in the Summer of 2018"

### Status Check:
- Need to verify if remapping completed
- If not, this remains a priority

### Recommendation:
- Complete citywide remapping by 2027
- Focus on high-growth neighborhoods first
- Update zoning to match existing use patterns (not aspirational patterns)

## IMPLEMENTATION ROADMAP

### Phase 1: Quick Wins (2026-2027) - Target 25% reduction in appeals

**Actions**:
1. Allow roof decks by-right in all residential zones
2. Eliminate parking minimums within 1/2 mile of transit
3. Increase height limits by 1 story in all residential zones
4. Create existing lot safe harbor for lot size non-conformance

**Expected impact**: ~250 fewer appeals/year (1,000 → 750)

### Phase 2: Major Reforms (2027-2028) - Target 50% reduction total

**Actions**:
5. Reduce minimum lot sizes across all RM/RSA zones
6. Reduce front/side/rear yard setbacks by 20-30%
7. Further reduce parking minimums (0.3:1 → 0.2:1)
8. Liberalize garage and fence requirements

**Expected impact**: ~250 additional fewer appeals/year (750 → 500)

### Phase 3: Comprehensive Update (2028-2029) - Target 70% reduction total

**Actions**:
9. Increase lot coverage allowances to 70-75%
10. Convert parking minimums to maximums in walkable areas
11. Simplify height measurement rules
12. Complete citywide remapping

**Expected impact**: ~200 additional fewer appeals/year (500 → 300)

## MEASURING SUCCESS

### Key Metrics to Track:

1. **By-Right Approval Rate**
   - Current: 80%
   - Phase 1 target: 85%
   - Phase 2 target: 90%
   - Phase 3 target: 95%

2. **Annual ZBA Appeals Volume**
   - Current: ~1,000/year
   - Phase 1 target: 750/year
   - Phase 2 target: 500/year
   - Phase 3 target: 300/year

3. **Variance Type Distribution**
   - Track whether reforms are addressing targeted issues
   - Monitor for unintended consequences

4. **Appeals by Zoning District**
   - Identify which zones still driving high variance activity
   - Target additional reforms to problematic zones

5. **Approval Timeline**
   - Measure time from application to approval
   - Goal: Reduce average time by 30%

## POLITICAL STRATEGY

### Building Coalition Support:

**Allies**:
- Philadelphia YIMBY (housing advocates)
- Homebuilders Association
- Affordable housing developers
- Transit advocacy groups
- Historic neighborhood groups (for context-based reforms)

**Potential Opposition**:
- Some RCOs (concerned about neighborhood character)
- Existing homeowners (concerned about property values)
- Historic preservation advocates

### Messaging:

**Frame reforms as**:
- "Reducing red tape for homeowners and small developers"
- "Making housing more affordable by reducing unnecessary costs"
- "Codifying what the ZBA already approves 90% of the time"
- "Matching zoning to how neighborhoods actually developed"

**Avoid framing as**:
- "Developer giveaway"
- "Eliminating community input"
- "Changing neighborhood character"

### Process:

1. **Build evidence base** - Use this analysis to show which rules drive unnecessary variance requests
2. **Pilot in willing districts** - Test reforms in 1-2 council districts first
3. **Show results** - Demonstrate reduced appeals, faster approvals, more housing
4. **Scale citywide** - Expand successful reforms across all zones
5. **Continuous improvement** - Monitor and adjust based on results

## PRECEDENTS FROM OTHER CITIES

### Minneapolis (2018-2020):
- **Eliminated parking minimums citywide** → 15% reduction in development costs
- **Allowed triplexes citywide** → Significant increase in housing production
- **Simplified ADU rules** → 5x increase in ADU permits

### Seattle (2019):
- **Backyard cottage/ADU reforms** → Permits increased from 50/year to 500/year
- **Height increases near transit** → 20% more housing capacity

### Austin (2021):
- **Reduced setbacks** → More buildable area on smaller lots
- **Increased lot coverage** → More feasible infill development

### Portland (2020):
- **Allowed fourplexes in single-family zones** → More "missing middle" housing
- **Eliminated parking requirements** → Reduced development costs 10-15%

## CONCLUSION

The data shows clear opportunities for reform:

✅ **Use variance reforms (2020) were a massive success** - 72% → 0% of appeals

🎯 **Next targets**: Open space/setbacks (30% of appeals), Height (50% of appeals), Accessory structures (44% of appeals)

📈 **Potential impact**: Could increase by-right rate from 80% to 95%+, reducing appeals from 1,000/year to 300/year

🏘️ **Housing production**: More predictable approvals = more housing built = lower costs

The 2018 PCPC report laid excellent groundwork. Now with 9 additional years of data, we can see which reforms worked (use variances) and which barriers remain (dimensional standards, height, accessory structures, parking).

**The path forward is clear: targeted code amendments to address the top 4 variance drivers.**
